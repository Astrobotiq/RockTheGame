# Wildcard Inspiration: Gravity-Held Acid Hazard (circle and rectangle)

Scope: far-field (non-game, non-sci-fi-film) inspiration for "a small machine holding a ring of deadly acid in the air with gravity". Each source is judged against Rock The Game: flat sky-blue background, orange ground with pink cream edges, plum pipes, **rainbow-ringed glowing grapple nodes (must NOT be confused with)**, bright green acid strips, red launch pads, pineapple collectibles, halftone print platforms, Bauhaus + brutalist UI, palette ink #140C1C / paper #F1E6C7 / red #FC1E00 / yellow #FCAE04 / blue #597DCE / sky #71BAEA / teal #289AA4 / pink #FF51DE / acid green.

Method note: 16 web searches (museum collections, NASA/JPL, ISO, Wikipedia, design history). Facts are cited; the concept cards themselves are design inferences, not facts.

## Q1. Which far-field sources can become concrete circle + rectangle acid-hazard concepts?

### Takeaway
En güçlü üç kaynak doğrudan "sıvıyı bir alanla tutma" fikrini taşıyor: **güneş gülü (Drosera)** bitkisinin asidik yapışkan damlaları, **Satürn halkasındaki elektrikle havada tutulan toz "spoke"ları** ve **tokamak'ın bükülmüş manyetik alanla plazmayı halkada tutması**. Bunlara Türk kimliği için **ters çevrilmiş nazar boncuğu**, okunabilirlik için **ISO 3864 sarı-siyah şerit** (bizim sarı #FCAE04, ISO sinyal sarısı #F9A900'a neredeyse eşit) ve Bauhaus uyumu için **Moholy-Nagy'nin delikli dönen diskleri** eklenmeli.

### Cited Findings

**Carnivorous plants: sundew (Drosera)**
- Sundew tentacles are stalked glands whose tips produce sticky mucilage droplets; the mucilage is an acidic (about pH 5) polysaccharide solution — [Wikipedia mirror: Drosera](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Drosera)
- Prey is attracted by light reflecting off the "dewdrops"; struggling triggers tentacles to bend inward and the leaf to curl; digestive enzymes are released into the mucilage — [Sundew (Wikipedia CD selection, EPFL)](https://dlab.epfl.ch/wikispeedia/wpcd/wp/s/Sundew.htm)
- A 2017 Naturwissenschaften NMR study describes Drosera capensis mucilage as highly viscous and elastic, the main means of prey capture — [Springer, Naturwissenschaften 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00114-017-1502-4)
- Sundews are used as a classroom lab subject — [SAPS: Using Sundews in the Lab](https://saps.org.uk/teaching-resources/resources/736/using-sundews-in-the-lab)

**Astronomy: Saturn's ring spokes**
- Cassini first imaged the B-ring spokes in 2005 (PIA07731, "Finally... Spokes!") — [JPL PIA07731](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia07731-finally-spokes/); [Photojournal PIA07731](https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA07731)
- Spoke groups can extend over more than 5,000 km radially (PIA08877 "Spoke Siblings") — [Photojournal PIA08877](https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA08877)
- Wedge-shaped spokes image (PIA10450 "Like Spokes of Old") — [JPL PIA10450](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia10450-like-spokes-of-old/)
- NASA's leading explanation: tiny dust grains acquire electric charge and **levitate above the rings**, dragged by Saturn's magnetic field; spokes come and go seasonally — per NASA Science ring feature summarised in search results (ring pages at [science.nasa.gov resource](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/finally-spokes)). Note: I did not open the NASA feature page directly; the wording came via search summary.

**Physics: tokamak (magnetic confinement)**
- "Tokamak" is a Russian acronym for "toroidal chamber with magnetic coils"; a toroidal field runs the long way round the torus and a poloidal field the short way, together forming a **twisted** field that confines the plasma — [DOE Explains... Tokamaks](https://www.energy.gov/node/4444676)
- ITER has computer-generated images of a toroidal plasma with particles following field lines, and a technical gallery of cross-sections — [ITER: What will ITER do?](https://www.iter.org/sci/goals); [ITER Technical Gallery](https://iter.org/node/18394); [ITER: The Tokamak](https://www.iter.org/node/2304)

**Turkish folk object: Nazar boncuğu**
- An eye-shaped amulet against the evil eye; "boncuk" = bead; likely originated in the Mediterranean alongside glassmaking — [Wikipedia: Nazar (amulet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazar_(amulet))
- Typical bead: handmade glass with concentric circles in dark blue, white, light blue and black, sometimes a yellow/gold edge; production concentrated near İzmir; made by lampworking coloured glass rods over a mandrel — [Turkey Travel Planner: Nazar Bonjuk](https://turkeytravelplanner.com/details/NazarBonjuk/index.html); [Anatolian Archaeology](https://www.anatolianarchaeology.net/?p=6754) (origin dates in lower-quality sources conflict; not used)

**Industrial signage: ISO 3864 and the biohazard trefoil**
- ISO 3864 (1984) allows safety yellow + black to mark temporary or permanent risk locations (collision, tripping, falling objects, holes) — [ISO 3864-1984 sample PDF](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/9454/f60dff81d94842b5988b657765f43a63/ISO-3864-1984.pdf)
- Warning sign = yellow triangle, black band and symbol, yellow ≥ 50% of area; warning yellow is RAL 1003 Signal Yellow ≈ #F9A900 — [Wikipedia: ISO 3864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3864); [ISO 3864-1:2011 catalogue](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/e38ba1f3-bbc1-4939-8df8-3078467e1f7c/iso-3864-1-2011)
- Risk-tier wording (yellow CAUTION vs orange WARNING in ISO 3864-2:2016) is reported by secondary sources; not verified against the standard — [Wikipedia: ISO 3864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3864)
- The biohazard symbol was developed at Dow Chemical in 1966 under Charles Baldwin; the brief was "memorable but meaningless" so people could be *taught* its meaning; 40+ candidates, six tested, the winner was the most remembered — [Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/collections/blog/the-biohazard-warning-symbol/); [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/article/biohazard-symbol-designed-to-be-memorable-but-meaningless/)

**Bauhaus kinetic art: Moholy-Nagy, Light Prop for an Electric Stage (1930)**
- One of the earliest electrically powered kinetic sculptures — [The Met collection](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/899299)
- A motor turns perforated discs and screens inside a box with a circular front opening, throwing moving patterns onto the wall — [Harvard Gazette 2007](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2007/07/light-prop-shines-again/); motion video: [Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/111474831); replicas: [Tate Papers 08](https://www.tate.org.uk/research/tate-papers/08/replicas-of-laszlo-moholy-nagys-light-prop-busch-reisinger-museum-and-harvard-university-art-museums); [metaLAB Harvard](https://metalab.harvard.edu/p/light-prop)

**Biology: Haeckel radiolaria; moon jellyfish**
- Kunstformen der Natur: 100 lithographic plates issued 1899–1904; Plate 11 = Discoidea (disc-shaped radiolaria with delicate silica skeletons), Plate 21 Acanthometra (spiny) — [Mediamatic plate pages](https://mediamatic.net/de/id/224285); plates idealised toward symmetry — [John Coulthart: Haeckel's Radiolaria](https://www.johncoulthart.com/feuilleton/2020/02/14/haeckels-radiolaria); full plate index: [Wikipedia: Kunstformen der Natur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstformen_der_Natur)
- Moon jellyfish (Aurelia aurita): almost entirely translucent bell with four horseshoe-shaped gonad rings, coloured red-magenta-blue (purple per Wildlife Trusts) — [Naturalis zooplankton](https://sat-zooplankton.linnaeus.naturalis.nl/linnaeus_ng/app/views/species/taxon.php?id=25681); [Wildlife Trusts](https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/node/3318)

**Lab spectacle: Van de Graaff and Tesla coils**
- Boston Museum of Science runs the world's largest air-insulated Van de Graaff (built at Dartmouth, build year disputed 1929/1933), discharging million-volt bolts with Tesla coils — [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/places/worlds-largest-airinsulated-van-de-graaff-generator); [MoS: Theater of Electricity](https://mos.org/inside-the-theater-of-electricity)
- Scienceworks Melbourne: giant Tesla coil > 2 million volts, 3 m bolts — [Museums Victoria Lightning Room](https://museumsvictoria.com.au/scienceworks/visiting/lightning-room)

**Instruments: armillary sphere**
- Nested pivoted rings around a centre; RMG holds brass, wooden and even interlocking paper-ring spheres — [RMG armillary sphere](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-10800); [RMG paper-ring sphere](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-19913)
- V&A gold ring with four pivoted hoops (England, 1750–1800) — [V&A M.368-1923](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O118457/ring-unknown)

**Kinetic sculpture: Calder**
- After visiting Mondrian's studio in 1930 Calder invented the mobile; Duchamp named them; some early mobiles were motor-driven — [Calder Foundation intro](https://calder.org/introduction/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Alexander-Calder/Mobiles-and-stabiles)
- Standing mobiles with a ball balanced against a counterweight: "Little Ball with Counterweight" (1931), "Two Spheres Within a Sphere" (1931), "Double Arc and Sphere" (1932) — [Calder: Little Ball with Counterweight](https://calder.org/works/standing-mobile/little-ball-with-counterweight-1931/); [Two Spheres Within a Sphere](https://calder.org/works/standing-mobile/two-spheres-within-a-sphere-1931); [Double Arc and Sphere](https://calder.org/works/standing-mobile/double-arc-and-sphere-1932)

**Atomic-age design: Googie**
- Futurist style from late-1940s Southern California influenced by car culture, jets, the Atomic and Space Ages; the starburst is a purely decorative "high-energy explosion" ornament; boomerangs as stylised flight/arrows — [Wikipedia: Googie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Googie_architecture); [Architizer: Googie 101](https://architizer.com/blog/practice/details/googie-101/)

**Toys: Japanese tin toys**
- Yoshiya's 1950s friction-driven tin "Atom Robot"; box advertised "mystery crank action" — [Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8538872/atom-robot)
- Yoshiya (Kobe Yoko) made fanciful wind-up toys for export in the 1950s–60s — [Smithsonian NASM "Space Elephant"](https://si.edu/es/object/toy-tin-toy-robot-space-elephant:nasm_A20060090001); industry declined with plastics and battery motors — [Journal of Antiques](https://journalofantiques.com/features/japanese-tin-toys-a-craze-that-rebuilt-post-war-japan/)

**Developer reference: Absolute Martian Manhunter (Javier Rodríguez)**
- Reviewers describe colour as the storytelling engine: a plain world becomes psychedelic around the protagonist, recalling 1960s music posters; ordinary scenes lose the strange colours when the Martian is absent — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/comics/marvel-comics/70-years-after-the-martian-manhunters-debut-the-justice-league-mainstay-has-been-reinvented-in-the-absolute-dc-universes-most-daring-and-compelling-series-yet); [Comic Watch #1](https://comic-watch.com/comic-book-reviews/absolute-martian-manhunter-1-written-in-full-color); [Comic Watch #8](https://comic-watch.com/comic-book-reviews/absolute-martian-manhunter-8-time-away/amp)

### Inferences (concept cards)

Cost scale (2D, solo dev): **S** = sprite + rotate/scale tween; **M** = shader (UV scroll / polar distortion) or 2-3 layered sprites; **L** = particles + custom shader + per-shape authoring.

**1. "Güneş Gülü Çekirdeği" — Sundew Core** (source: Drosera)
- Pitch: a mechanical core bristling with short stalks; every stalk tip holds a trembling bead of acid, and the beads together form the "ring".
- Circle: ink/plum hub; 10–16 radial pin-stalks; acid-green droplets on tips with a white specular dot (the "glisten" that lures prey). Droplets wobble out of phase.
- Rectangle: a brass bar like a comb/hairbrush, stalks on all four sides; droplets along the edges stretch slightly in the motion direction.
- Lethal read: very strong — radiating spikes + wet drops = "sticky, will eat you"; and the real biology is literally acidic, a nice lore hook. Risk: low; bead silhouettes are spiky, not round like rainbow nodes.
- Cost: S–M (one stalk+drop sprite instanced radially; a sine wobble on scale).

**2. "Spoke Halkası" — Levitated Spoke Ring** (source: Saturn B-ring spokes, charged dust levitated by a field)
- Pitch: the acid is not a solid ring but a field of droplets held aloft, with darker radial "spokes" sweeping through as the field pulses.
- Circle: small dense core; flat acid disc made of halftone dots (fits the platform look!) with 2–3 dark wedge spokes rotating at a different speed from the disc.
- Rectangle: a racetrack (stadium) band of halftone acid dots around a core bar; spokes become perpendicular dark bands travelling along the length.
- Lethal read: medium-strong; the counter-rotating spokes say "machine at work". Risk: a smooth disc can read as a platform or a ring target — keep the outer edge ragged/dripping, never a clean rainbow band.
- Cost: M (one polar-UV shader: halftone dots + wedge mask scroll). Reuses the existing halftone shader knowledge.

**3. "Tokamak Simidi" — Twisted-Field Torus** (source: tokamak toroidal + poloidal = twisted field)
- Pitch: the acid ring is visibly wound with helical "field" stripes, as if a magnet bottle is twisting it in place.
- Circle: acid torus seen from the front with diagonal ink stripes scrolling around it (barber-pole on a ring); small coil-blocks clamped at 3 or 4 points (Toriyama-style bolts/rivets).
- Rectangle: a racetrack/stadium torus around a core bar, coils at the corners; helical stripes travel along the long sides.
- Lethal read: strong — scrolling diagonals read as "energised/contained under pressure" and echo hazard stripes. Risk: striped ring could resemble a candy/simit (fits the cream-candy ground, but softens menace). Counter with ink + yellow stripes, not white.
- Cost: M (UV scroll shader on a ring/stadium mesh or 9-sliced sprite).

**4. "Ters Nazar" — Inverted Evil Eye** (source: nazar boncuğu)
- Pitch: the Turkish eye bead turned malevolent — acid green where cobalt should be, an ink pupil that is the machine core, and it *tracks the player*.
- Circle: concentric rings: ink core (pupil, mechanical iris blades) → paper ring → acid-green ring → thin yellow edge (the traditional gold edge). Pupil slides toward the player.
- Rectangle: an almond/lens-shaped "eye" stretched along the bar, or a row of 3 eyes on a bar like a beaded nazar string.
- Lethal read: medium. Risk: HIGH — concentric rings are exactly the grapple-node vocabulary, and nazar is culturally *protective/lucky* (could read as a charm/collectible). Only use if the eye tracking + acid drips make it unmistakably hostile; never add rainbow or glow halo.
- Cost: S–M (layered circles + pupil offset toward player).

**5. "Sarı-Siyah Bant" — Hazard-Tape Core** (source: ISO 3864)
- Pitch: whatever the outer acid does, the machine core is always wrapped in yellow/black diagonal tape — a universal "do not touch".
- Circle: core disc with yellow #FCAE04/ink #140C1C 45° stripes; acid ring around it.
- Rectangle: the core bar IS hazard tape; acid wraps it like a sleeve.
- Lethal read: strongest real-world convention; our yellow #FCAE04 is near RAL 1003 #F9A900, so it is already in-palette. Risk: very low; could feel too "industrial-safe", but it can be the *grammar layer* combined with any other concept.
- Cost: S (one tiling stripe texture or a stripe shader).

**6. "Kendi Trefoil'imiz" — Teach-it Glyph** (source: biohazard symbol's "memorable but meaningless" brief)
- Pitch: don't borrow a real symbol; invent one game-owned 3-lobed acid glyph and teach it once (first room) — every acid hazard carries it.
- Circle: three acid lobes held around a core by three clamp-arms, rotating; the gaps between lobes create readable rhythm.
- Rectangle: lobes become repeated half-circle acid bulges along the bar edge.
- Lethal read: depends on teaching; Baldwin's lesson is that memorability beats literal meaning. Risk: a trefoil could read as a fidget-spinner/toy; mitigate with drips and sound.
- Cost: S (sprite) — the cost is level-design teaching, not art.

**7. "Moholy Diski" — Perforated Disc Modulator** (source: Moholy-Nagy Light Prop)
- Pitch: a Bauhaus machine of perforated metal discs turning at different speeds; acid seeps through the holes and is flung outward.
- Circle: two stacked perforated discs (paper + ink) counter-rotating over an acid disc; acid shows only through the holes, creating moiré flicker.
- Rectangle: perforated plates sliding past each other along the bar (like a mechanical shutter), acid oozing at the edges.
- Lethal read: medium; the flicker reads as "active machinery". Matches the Bauhaus UI direction strongly. Risk: perforated disc may read as a speaker/vent (neutral), so acid must spill past the outline.
- Cost: M (two masked sprites rotating; moiré comes free).

**8. "Radyolarya Çarkı" — Radiolarian Disc** (source: Haeckel Plate 11 Discoidea / 21 Acanthometra)
- Pitch: the acid disc is held in a lacy silica-like cage with long spines; the cage is the gravity device.
- Circle: ink lattice disc with radial spines; acid fills the lattice cells like stained glass.
- Rectangle: a spined lattice bar (like a sea-urchin baguette).
- Lethal read: strong — spines + organic symmetry feel alien and sharp. Fits the "scanned illustration" NPC world (both are printed natural-history imagery). Risk: ornate detail may mush at speed; keep spines few and thick.
- Cost: M (hand-drawn lattice sprite; public-domain Haeckel plates can be traced for reference).

**9. "Ay Denizanası" — Moon-Jelly Bell** (source: Aurelia aurita)
- Pitch: a translucent acid bell with four bright horseshoe "organs" that are actually the machine's gravity coils.
- Circle: semi-transparent acid disc, four pink/magenta horseshoes around a tiny core; slow pulsing scale.
- Rectangle: a long jelly sleeve with the horseshoes repeated along it.
- Lethal read: weak-medium. Risk: HIGH friendly/cute read; pink overlaps player pink #FF51DE. Better as an *ambient variant* in a water-themed world than the default hazard.
- Cost: S–M (alpha sprite + pulse).

**10. "Van de Graaff Kubbesi" — Sparking Dome** (source: Van de Graaff + Tesla coil shows)
- Pitch: a polished dome core; the acid ring is held by crackling arcs that jump from the dome to the acid.
- Circle: paper/chrome dome with halftone shading; 3–5 short jagged ink/yellow arcs flicker between core and acid ring.
- Rectangle: a row of small domes along a bar, arcs bridging to the acid sleeve.
- Lethal read: very strong — electricity is a universal "touch = die" sign; arcs can also act as a *telegraph* (arcs intensify before speed-up). Risk: conflates acid and electricity (two hazard types in one); decide which kills.
- Cost: M–L (procedural lightning via LineRenderer jitter or flipbook).

**11. "Armil Kafes" — Armillary Gimbal** (source: armillary spheres)
- Pitch: the core sits in nested brass hoops that tumble on different axes; acid is smeared on the hoops.
- Circle: 2–3 ellipse hoops (2D fake rotation by scaling X/Y) around a core.
- Rectangle: a gimbal frame — rectangular hoops pivoting around the bar.
- Lethal read: medium-weak. Risk: HIGH — nested rings around a centre is the closest silhouette to the rainbow grapple nodes, and reads as precious/collectible (it is literally jewellery in the V&A). Not recommended as default.
- Cost: S (scale tweens).

**12. "Calder Terazisi" — Counterweight Mobile** (source: Calder standing mobiles)
- Pitch: the hazard is a balanced arm: core pivot, acid blob on one end, counterweight on the other — the acid blob is what kills.
- Circle: small pivot + a big acid sphere and a small ink counterweight on a thin wire, the whole thing swinging.
- Rectangle: a long beam balanced on a pivot, acid-coated beam ends.
- Lethal read: medium; motion (pendulum swing) adds new play patterns for a grappling game. Risk: reads as decorative art; rely on acid colour.
- Cost: S–M (hinge joint or tween; arguably a new hazard *type* rather than a skin).

**13. "Googie Patlaması" — Atomic Starburst Core** (source: Googie starburst)
- Pitch: an atomic-age starburst core spitting acid, like a 1950s motel sign gone toxic.
- Circle: spiky starburst ink core, acid ring around, small boomerang fins orbiting.
- Rectangle: a boomerang-shaped bar with a starburst at the centre.
- Lethal read: medium — spikes help. Risk: starburst = "sparkle/reward" in many visual languages; could look like a collectible pickup. Use for structure, not as main silhouette.
- Cost: S.

**14. "Teneke Kurmalı" — Tin Wind-up Machine** (source: Japanese tin toys, friction "mystery crank action")
- Pitch: the gravity device is a lithographed tin toy with a turning wind-up key; it runs down and rewinds, telegraphing its speed changes.
- Circle: printed-tin core with rivets and a visible key on the back turning; acid ring around.
- Rectangle: a tin bar with a key at one end; speed visibly tied to key rotation.
- Lethal read: medium; the toy charm softens it but the visible mechanism gives a clear *timing tell* (key slows = hazard slowing). Strong fit with Toriyama-style chunky mechanics (inference; not researched here). Risk: toy = friendly — acid must dominate silhouette.
- Cost: S–M (key sprite rotation linked to movement speed).

**15. Colour layer: "Marslı Renk Kayması"** (source: Absolute Martian Manhunter colour)
- Not a shape, a rule: hazards are the only objects whose colour is "wrong" for the world — when near/active they shift into psychedelic acid hues, the way Rodríguez lets strange colour mark the uncanny.
- Circle/rectangle: a palette swap/hue cycle on the acid layer only (green → yellow → acid-pink edges), core stays ink.
- Cost: S (shader hue cycle or gradient map).

### Gaps
- I did not research Turkish foods (simit, lokum), Memphis, constructivism, Soviet space posters, Op-art, snow globes, centrifuges or lava lamps in this pass (tool budget); the tokamak and spoke concepts cover the "ring held by a field" idea that those would have served.
- Akira Toriyama's mechanical drawings were not researched; references to "Toriyama-style rivets/bolts" are inference from the brief.
- NASA's spoke explanation came via a search summary of a NASA Science page I did not open directly.
- No source tests whether these silhouettes are confused with grapple nodes in play; that requires a playtest.

## Q2. What reads as lethal at speed, and what risks reading as friendly/collectible?

### Takeaway
Ölümcül okumayı en çok **sivri dış hat, ıslak/damlayan kenar, sarı-siyah şerit ve elektrik arkı** sağlıyor; dost/collectible okumasının en büyük riski **iç içe temiz halkalar, parıltı haleleri ve şeker/oyuncak sevimliliği**. Grapple node'ları gökkuşağı halkalı ve parlak olduğu için asit tehlikesi asla temiz eşmerkezli halka + glow kombinasyonu kullanmamalı.

### Cited Findings
- Yellow + black is a standardised marker for risk locations and warning signs, with yellow ≥ 50% of a warning sign — [ISO 3864-1984 sample](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/9454/f60dff81d94842b5988b657765f43a63/ISO-3864-1984.pdf); [Wikipedia: ISO 3864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3864)
- A warning symbol can be *taught*; Baldwin's team prioritised memorability and tested recall against 36 common symbols — [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/article/biohazard-symbol-designed-to-be-memorable-but-meaningless/); [Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/collections/blog/the-biohazard-warning-symbol/)
- Sundew droplets glisten to *lure* prey — glisten is an attractor, not a deterrent — [Sundew](https://dlab.epfl.ch/wikispeedia/wpcd/wp/s/Sundew.htm)
- The nazar is a protective/good-luck amulet — [Wikipedia: Nazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazar_(amulet))
- Armillary forms were made as jewellery (finger rings) — [V&A M.368-1923](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O118457/ring-unknown); [British Museum AF.1761](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_AF-1761)
- Googie starburst is a purely decorative ornament — [Wikipedia: Googie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Googie_architecture)

### Inferences
- **Lethal cues (stack 2+):** spiky/irregular silhouette (sundew, radiolarian, starburst), wet dripping edge, yellow/ink diagonal stripes on the core, flickering arcs, visible counter-rotating machinery.
- **Friendly cues to avoid:** clean concentric rings (armillary, nazar, plain Saturn ring) — closest to grapple nodes; soft glow halo — nodes glow; candy stripes in white/pink — reads as food; pink near #FF51DE — reads as player-owned; jewellery-like brass — reads as collectible.
- The sundew lesson cuts both ways: a glistening highlight *attracts* the eye — good for noticing at speed, bad if it looks like a pickup. Keep the specular dot small and pair it with spikes.
- A hard rule for the art bible: **grapple target = rainbow + glow + clean ring; hazard = acid green + ink + yellow/black + ragged edge + no glow halo.** Nazar and armillary concepts violate this by default.
- Teaching (biohazard lesson): whichever concept wins, introduce it in a safe first encounter so the glyph is learned, not assumed.

### Gaps
- No empirical data on silhouette-confusion at the game's actual camera speed; needs a greybox playtest with gamepad (game feel is the human's call per project rules).

## Q3. Implementation cost and recommended shortlist

### Takeaway
Tek kişilik ekip için en iyi oran: **Güneş Gülü (S–M) + Sarı-Siyah Bant çekirdek (S)** varsayılan tehlike olarak; **Tokamak Simidi** veya **Spoke Halkası** (M, tek shader) dikdörtgen/racetrack versiyonu için. **Van de Graaff arkları** hız değişimini önceden haber veren (telegraph) katman olarak sonraya bırakılabilir. Nazar, armil ve denizanası grapple node/collectible ile karışma riski yüzünden varsayılan olmamalı; nazar yalnızca oyuncuyu takip eden göz bebeğiyle ve özel bir Türk-temalı dünyada denenmeli.

### Cited Findings
- The project already uses a halftone print look; the Saturn-spoke concept can be expressed as halftone dots on a polar UV, and Haeckel plates are public-domain 1899–1904 lithographs suitable as tracing references — [Kunstformen der Natur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstformen_der_Natur); [Mediamatic plates](https://mediamatic.net/de/id/224285)
- Moholy-Nagy's effect is produced by perforated discs rotating at different speeds — i.e., two masked rotating layers — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2007/07/light-prop-shines-again/)
- Tokamak confinement is visually expressible as two field directions combining into a twist — [DOE Explains... Tokamaks](https://www.energy.gov/node/4444676)
